The History Of Marine Suplies

Prehistoric & Ancient Eras: The earliest known watercraft date back over 10,000 years. Early civilization networks, such as those in the Aegean Sea and the vast Austronesian expansion across the Pacific and Indian Oceans, relied heavily on advanced canoe and catamaran navigation. • The Age of Discovery (15th–17th Centuries): Driven by commercial trade and colonization, European explorers like Christopher Columbus and John Cabot charted global ocean routes, permanently linking global economies. • The Industrial & Modern Eras: The transition from wooden sailing ships to ironclad steamships in the 19th century revolutionized global commerce and warfare. Today, maritime history focuses on the containerization of global shipping and marine environmental history.>

Marine Communication Equipments

• VHF (Very High Frequency) Radios: The primary short-range communication device operating between 156–174 MHz with a line-of-sight range of about 20 to 30 nautical miles. Uses Channel 16 for international distress and safety, and Channel 70 for Digital Selective Calling (DSC). Available in fixed-mount and handheld portable options. • MF/HF (Medium Frequency / High Frequency) Radios: Used for long-distance and ocean-crossing voice communication, Single Sideband (SSB) radio, and DSC/NBDP alerts (operating on frequencies like 2182 kHz for distress). • Satellite Communication Terminals (Inmarsat / Iridium / Fleet Broadband): Provide global, beyond-the-horizon coverage for voice calls, high-speed data, internet, and emergency messaging where terrestrial radio signals cannot reach. • Automatic Identification System (AIS): Transmits and receives real-time vessel data—including identity, position, course, speed, and destination—over VHF maritime bands to assist with collision avoidance and tracking.
